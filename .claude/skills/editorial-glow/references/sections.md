# sections

How a section is put together in this style. Where its layout comes from — a
drawn reference or the brief — is the build process's call (`site-brief`, or a
template's House rules), not this file's.

## The header block

Every section opens on the same three lines, left-aligned on the content edge:
the eyebrow (brand dot + two to four uppercase words), the serif heading, one
line of lede. Then 64–96px of white, then the section's one body.

A section whose figure is centred (a statement, a single quote) may centre the
block above it. Nothing else centres.

## One body per section

The body is one of:

- **a joined panel** — plans, features, a comparison, a spec table;
- **a figure** — a line drawing, a product mock, a data dial, a framed
  photograph;
- **a split** — a figure beside one panel or a short list, unequal widths
  (5/7, 4/8), the figure allowed to crop off the far edge;
- **a list with a figure** — vertical tabs on the left, the chosen item's
  figure in the middle, its one-paragraph explanation on the right (gru's
  "One message").

Two bodies in one section is two sections.

## Rhythm across a page

- Alternate a panel section with a figure section; two panels in a row read as
  a document, not a page.
- A figure cropped by the viewport happens once or twice a page, on the side
  the copy is not.

## Voice

The studio's copy rules apply (`soft-neutral/references/copy.md`). This style
adds:

- Headings are short declaratives with a turn: "Built different", "Your day,
  fully handled", "One message. Infinite possibilities." Not slogans stacked
  with adjectives.
- The eyebrow names the section's job in two to four words ("Why us",
  "Pricing", "A day with …").
- The lede is one sentence that makes the heading concrete; it never repeats
  it.
- Footnotes are allowed and good: a `*` or `†` on a claim, defined under the
  panel in one line each.

## Floor

- Every section has its header block and exactly one body.
- No two adjacent sections share a body type.
- Copy never crops; figures may.
